# 13.3 Cross-Cultural & Inclusive Design

## In one line

Your defaults encode your context — most "universal" design decisions are actually local ones you have stopped noticing.

## Localisation vs. internationalisation vs. transcreation

- **Internationalisation (i18n)** — building so the product *can* adapt: externalised strings, locale-aware formatting, flexible layout, RTL support.
- **Localisation (l10n)** — adapting to a specific locale: translation, formats, imagery, payment methods, legal requirements.
- **Transcreation** — rewriting for cultural resonance rather than literal accuracy; usually needed for marketing and onboarding copy.

## What actually breaks

| Area | Failure |
| --- | --- |
| **Text expansion** | German and Finnish run ~30% longer than English; fixed-width buttons break |
| **RTL languages** | Arabic, Hebrew, Urdu, Persian mirror layout, navigation direction, and icon direction; progress moves right-to-left |
| **Names** | "First/Last name" fails for mononyms, patronymics, multiple surnames, and name order differences; use a single full-name field where possible |
| **Addresses** | Postcode format, state/province presence, house-numbering conventions; forcing a US shape excludes real users |
| **Phone numbers** | Length and format vary; never validate to a single pattern |
| **Dates and numbers** | DD/MM vs MM/DD ambiguity, decimal separators, digit grouping (Indian lakh/crore grouping differs from Western thousands) |
| **Calendars** | Non-Gregorian calendars, different week start days, local holidays |
| **Currency** | Symbol position, decimal conventions, and rounding expectations |
| **Colour meaning** | Red/green financial conventions invert in parts of East Asia; white signals mourning in some cultures |
| **Icons** | Mailboxes, trash cans, gestures, hand symbols, and animals carry local meaning; the thumbs-up is not universally positive |
| **Imagery** | Representation of people, dress, and family structures |
| **Payment** | Cards dominate in some markets, UPI, wallets, bank transfer, or cash-on-delivery in others |
| **Connectivity and devices** | Low bandwidth, intermittent connectivity, older Android devices, and data cost are the normal condition for a large share of the world |

## Hofstede's cultural dimensions — use with care

Power distance, individualism/collectivism, uncertainty avoidance, masculinity/femininity, long-term orientation, indulgence/restraint. Useful as a **prompt for questions**, dangerous as a description of individuals: national averages say nothing about the person in front of you, and the research has well-known methodological criticisms. Use it to generate hypotheses to test locally, never as a substitute for local research.

## Designing beyond your own context

- **Test in the actual context.** Bandwidth, device, language, lighting, and interruption patterns matter more than most interface details.
- **Recruit locally.** Diaspora participants are not a substitute for in-market users.
- **Work with local partners** for language and cultural review; machine translation of UI strings without review produces embarrassing and sometimes offensive results.
- **Check the whole journey**, including payment, delivery, support hours, and legal disclosures — the failure is usually outside the app.
- **Design for the low end** — a product that works on a 3-year-old mid-range Android on a slow connection works everywhere; the reverse is not true.

## Inclusive design beyond geography

- **Names, gender, and honorifics** — offer free text or an inclusive option set, and ask only when you genuinely need it.
- **Family and relationship structures** — "spouse", "parent", "household" encode assumptions.
- **Literacy and numeracy** — plain language, icons paired with text, and avoiding tables of numbers as the only representation.
- **Financial situation** — data cost, device limits, and the tone of debt- or fee-related messaging.
- **Age** — larger targets and text, tolerance for slower interaction, and familiarity with fewer conventions.

## Interview angle

**Common questions**
- "How would you adapt this product for a new market?"
- "What do you consider when designing for a global audience?"

**Model answer:** "I would separate what has to be built for adaptability from what has to be researched locally. On the build side: externalised strings, layouts that survive 30% text expansion, RTL support, locale-aware dates, numbers, currency, names and addresses, and performance targets set for a mid-range device on a slow connection. On the research side, I would not assume: I would recruit in-market users, test in the real context, and check the whole journey including payment methods, delivery expectations, and support — most of the failures I have seen were outside the interface. And I would treat frameworks like Hofstede's as a source of questions to ask locally, not as answers about individuals."
