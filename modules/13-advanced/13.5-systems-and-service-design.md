# 13.5 Systems & Service Design

## In one line

Most experiences are delivered by a system of people, policies, and channels — the screen is one touchpoint, and often not the one that is broken.

## Service design basics

**A service blueprint** extends a journey map downward through the layers:

```
Physical evidence      (what the user sees/receives)
─────────────────────────────────────────────
User actions           (what the user does)
─────────────── line of interaction ─────────
Frontstage actions     (staff/system actions the user sees)
─────────────── line of visibility ──────────
Backstage actions      (staff/system actions the user does not see)
─────────────── line of internal interaction ─
Support processes      (systems, policies, third parties)
```

Its value is that it locates a failure at the right layer. A "confusing status page" is often a backstage problem: the status is genuinely unknown because two systems do not reconcile. No amount of interface work fixes that, and the blueprint makes the real fix visible and assignable.

## Channels and continuity

Users move between app, web, email, SMS, phone support, and physical locations. The most common failures are at the seams:

- Information collected in one channel is not available in the next ("please repeat your reference number").
- State diverges — the app says shipped, the email says delayed.
- Tone diverges — friendly in-app, bureaucratic in email.
- The recovery channel is the worst-designed one, which is exactly backwards: people reach support at their most frustrated (Peak–End, Module 03).

**Design the seams deliberately.** Continuity across channels is usually worth more than polish within one.

## Second-order effects

Every design change causes effects beyond the intended one. Ask, routinely:

- **Who else is affected?** Support staff, moderators, sellers, admins, family members sharing an account.
- **What behaviour does this incentivise?** Any visible metric will be gamed by someone.
- **What happens at scale?** A nudge that is fine for one user can be a systemic effect at millions.
- **What happens when it fails?** Not only "if the request errors" but "if the whole channel is down."
- **What does this make impossible later?** Some designs foreclose structural options.

## Internal tools and the invisible users

Support agents, moderators, warehouse staff, clinicians, and back-office teams are users with high frequency, high expertise, and enormous aggregate time cost. Design there is unusually high-leverage and unusually neglected, and the metrics (time saved × loaded cost × headcount) are easy to compute (Module 12.2). If you want measurable impact quickly in a large organisation, internal tools are often the fastest route.

## Systems thinking tools

- **Causal loop diagrams** — surface reinforcing and balancing feedback loops (more users → more content → more users).
- **Stock and flow thinking** — distinguish the level (retained users) from the rate (signups per week); most teams optimise flows and ignore stocks.
- **Leverage points** — the highest-leverage interventions are usually rules, goals, and information flows, not surface parameters. In product terms: what gets measured and what is default beats what colour the button is.
- **Unintended consequence scanning** — before shipping, ask each stakeholder group how they could be harmed or how they could game it.

## Interview angle

**Common questions**
- "How do you design for an experience that spans channels?"
- "Tell me about a time the fix was not in the interface."
- "What would you consider beyond the screen?"

**For the last:** "Policy, timing, and the other people in the system. If a user is anxious about a delayed order, the fix might be a status page — but it might equally be a refund policy change, a proactive notification, or fixing why the two systems disagree about the status. I would map the service, not just the screen, because the emotional low in most journeys sits in the gaps between channels, and that is where a small change moves satisfaction more than anything I can do inside the app."
