# How to Use This Repo

## The three modes

**Mode 1 — Learn.** Read a module top to bottom. Do not skip the "Applications" section; concepts you cannot point at in a real product are concepts you cannot defend in an interview.

**Mode 2 — Drill.** Take the quiz at the end of each module *before* re-reading it. Retrieval practice beats re-reading by a wide margin for retention. Getting a question wrong and then looking it up is the point, not a failure.

**Mode 3 — Perform.** Work through `interview-prep/`. Every answer should be spoken out loud, ideally recorded. Reading an answer and saying an answer are different skills, and interviews test the second one.

## A working loop that fits in a day

1. **20 min** — read one concept file.
2. **10 min** — open a real product on your phone and find the concept in the wild. Screenshot it. Write two sentences on whether it was applied well.
3. **10 min** — answer the "Interview angle" prompt out loud, timed to 90 seconds.
4. **5 min** — add the concept to your flashcards (`quizzes/flashcards.md`).

Four items a week beats twenty items you never revisit.

## Rules that make this stick

- **Always name the trade-off.** Every UX principle costs something. Progressive disclosure reduces cognitive load but adds clicks. Consistency aids learnability but blocks innovation. An answer that names the cost sounds senior; an answer that treats a principle as free sounds junior.
- **Attach a number where one exists.** "Doherty threshold, 400 ms" beats "fast feedback matters."
- **Never cite a law you cannot apply.** Interviewers probe: "Okay, where would you use that here?" Have the application ready before you have the name ready.
- **Critique before you design.** Judging an existing screen is the fastest way to build design vocabulary, and app critique is itself a common interview round.
